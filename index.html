A note from the GALILEO team

GALILEO is our practical way to explore the night sky through engineering. With Prof. Khaled Youssef as our mentor, we are combining mechanics, electronics, astronomy, AI, and software to build an autonomous smart telescope.

The project helps students, educators, researchers, and astronomy enthusiasts learn, demonstrate ideas, and observe targets more easily. Our software connects the app, sensors, camera, and telescope so different people can use the system in a clear and useful way.

The big picture

Following the storyboard: a target is picked in the app, the controller works out where to point, motors move, the camera looks, AI checks what it sees, and the loop corrects itself.

User picks target (Flutter)→Command sent over Wi-Fi→Controller receives→Compute Az/Alt→Motors slew→Camera captures→YOLO detects→Track & adjust↺

Prototype vs final architecture

Two different machines on one roadmap. Learn on the prototype first; design toward the final one.

built today

Current prototype

  • Arduino UNO as the single controller (5 V logic)
  • A4988 drivers, up to 1/16 microstepping, audible motor noise
  • NEMA 17 steppers on Azimuth and Elevation
  • Serial commands, limit switches, manual testing
  • Goal: prove two-axis motion, homing and degrees-to-steps maths
proposed final

Final GALILEO system

  • ESP32 real-time controller (3.3 V, Wi-Fi) driving motors
  • TMC2209 silent drivers with UART configuration
  • Raspberry Pi 5 + HQ camera: YOLOv11, plate solving, tracking, logging
  • FastAPI backend with SQLite/Firebase, Flutter app (Android & iOS)
  • Sensors: GPS NEO-6M, MPU6050 IMU, QMC5883L compass, limit switches, encoders; 12 V / 5 A power with buck converters

Every phase below carries a badge: prototype final both so nobody studies the wrong board by accident.

References and inspiration

Our project was inspired by several open-source telescope and observatory projects. They helped us explore telescope control, automation, mechanical design, and the integration of embedded systems with astronomy.

1. Arduino Telescope Control

A practical Arduino-based telescope control project that inspired our motor control and telescope positioning.

github.com/juanrmn/Arduino-Telescope-Control

2. Pi-lomar 3D Printed Working Miniature Observatory

An inspiring project combining 3D printing, Raspberry Pi, and mechanical automation to create a functional miniature observatory.

instructables.com: Pi-lomar

3. ROS Observatory

An open-source observatory project showing how sensors, motors, software, and astronomical equipment can be integrated for automated operation.

github.com/fdomf/ROS-observatory

4. Autoguided Telescope

A practical telescope automation project that provides additional inspiration for building and guiding an autonomous telescope system.

instructables.com: Autoguided Telescope

5. Control Your Telescope Using Stellarium & Arduino

A step-by-step DIY telescope controller that connects Stellarium with Arduino. It demonstrates Altitude/Azimuth position sensing with incremental encoders, coordinate conversion to Right Ascension and Declination, and communication through the LX200 protocol. This is a useful reference for GALILEO’s telescope positioning and software-control workflow.

instructables.com: Stellarium & Arduino Telescope Control

These projects were valuable references throughout our development, helping us learn from existing solutions while building our own autonomous telescope system.
